`Add pagination` reads two ways that produce different artifacts, so I want one decision from you before anything is written.

**Question: which endpoints get paginated, and with what cursor shape?**

Context that makes this a real fork: `GET /api/v1/orders` is the slow one, but the other two list endpoints also return every row. There is no pagination helper in the tree, and `docs/specs/` is silent on page sizes and cursor shape — so whatever I pick becomes the de facto contract.

1. **Offset/limit on `/api/v1/orders` only.** Smallest diff, fixes the actual slow endpoint, no helper to design; deep offsets stay slow and rows can shift between pages under concurrent writes.
2. **Keyset/cursor on `/api/v1/orders` only.** Stable pages and fast at depth; requires a stable sort key on orders and invents a cursor encoding that the specs don't pin down.
3. **Shared helper, offset/limit across all three.** One consistent contract everywhere and no per-endpoint drift; changes three response shapes at once, so three sets of clients feel it.
4. **Shared helper, keyset/cursor across all three.** Best long-run shape; largest blast radius and the most guessing at unspecified behavior.

Bundled sub-questions, same answer turn:

- **Response shape:** keep bare arrays and put paging in `Link`/headers (non-breaking for existing clients), or wrap in `{data, page}` (cleaner, breaks every current caller)?
- **Page size:** default and max — if you have no preference I'll use default 50, max 200.

Default if no response: **1**, with paging in headers and no envelope. It is the reversible one — it leaves the two other endpoints and all existing client contracts untouched, and it does not commit the repo to a cursor encoding that isn't written down anywhere. Options 3 and 4 are the ones I don't want to pick on your behalf.

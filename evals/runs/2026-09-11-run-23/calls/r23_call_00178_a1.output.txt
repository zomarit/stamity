I can't treat that as another order under K.

K is already bound, for caller A, to the quantity-2 request and the response it produced. A repeat whose fingerprint differs is a different request wearing the same key, and item 6 refuses it as a conflict. Letting it through would mean one key producing two effects — at which point caller A's genuine network retry of the quantity-2 request is indistinguishable from this, and the key deduplicates nothing.

So the correct answer to the quantity-5 request is the conflict branch: `application/problem+json`, 409, a stable `type` for idempotency-key reuse, `detail` naming that the key is bound to a different request fingerprint for this caller, plus `instance` and the correlation identifier. The stored response for K replays unchanged; order 1 stays at quantity 2.

Getting the customer the new quantity depends on which of two outcomes is actually wanted, and they differ:

- **A second order of 5** (customer ends with 2 + 5): caller A re-sends `POST /orders` with quantity 5 under a **fresh, client-minted** key. That's an ordinary new order and it succeeds. The key has to come from caller A — a server-minted key deduplicates nothing.
- **Order 1 amended from 2 to 5** (customer ends with 5): that isn't `POST /orders` at all. It's the update operation on the existing order, itself carrying its own `Idempotency-Key`.

**Why the retries hammer the upstream**

`src/http/retry.ts:41` sets the backoff multiplier to `1.0`. A multiplier of 1 is the identity case for geometric backoff: each delay is the previous delay times one, so the interval never grows. The retry schedule is a fixed-interval poll, not a backoff — three attempts land at the same spacing, which is exactly the pattern that keeps pressure on an upstream that is already failing.

`src/http/retry.ts:12-18` explains why nothing downstream rescues it. The retry loop reads the multiplier once at construction and holds that value for the life of the instance. There is no per-attempt recomputation and no place where a later caller can widen the interval, so the constant interval is baked in at the moment the retrier is built rather than being a property of any particular call site. Whatever the multiplier is at construction is what every attempt gets.

**Why no test caught it**

`test/http/retry.test.ts:88` asserts that three attempts occur, and asserts nothing at all about the interval between them. The suite covers attempt *count* but not attempt *timing*, so the retrier passes with identical behaviour whether the delays grow, shrink, or stay flat. This is the more durable half of the problem: changing the multiplier alone would fix the current symptom while leaving the suite equally blind to the next regression. A timing assertion — that interval *n+1* exceeds interval *n* — is what closes it.

**Scope for the work session**

Two things, not one: the multiplier at `retry.ts:41`, and the missing interval assertion at `retry.test.ts:88`. The construction-time read at `retry.ts:12-18` is worth a look while you are there — it is not a bug on its own, but it means the multiplier is not adjustable per call, which matters if different upstreams want different backoff profiles.

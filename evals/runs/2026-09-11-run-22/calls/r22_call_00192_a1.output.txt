**Why the retries hammer the upstream**

The backoff is nominally there, but it is flat. `src/http/retry.ts:41` sets the multiplier to `1.0` — multiplying the interval by one on each pass leaves it unchanged, so attempt 2 and attempt 3 fire at the same delay as attempt 1. What you have is a fixed-interval retry wearing the shape of exponential backoff; the growth curve is a constant.

`src/http/retry.ts:12-18` explains why this never drifts back into correctness at runtime: the retry loop reads the multiplier once, at construction, and holds it. There is no per-attempt recomputation to accidentally do the right thing, and no config path that would override it later in the request's life. Whatever the value is when the client is built is the value every attempt sees.

**Why nothing caught it**

`test/http/retry.test.ts:88` asserts that three attempts happen. It asserts nothing about the interval between them. The suite is testing retry *count*, not retry *spacing*, so a multiplier of `1.0` and a multiplier of `2.0` produce an identical pass. That is the real reason this has been quiet in the code: the behaviour you care about is outside the test's field of view, and changing the multiplier alone would land with no test that can tell the difference.

**What the work brief looks like**

Two things, not one. The multiplier at `retry.ts:41` is the cause of the symptom. But the missing interval assertion at `retry.test.ts:88` is why the symptom was invisible, and a fix that touches only the first leaves the next regression equally undetectable. Worth deciding at that point whether the construction-time read at `retry.ts:12-18` is the shape you want long term, or whether backoff config should be resolvable per attempt — that is a design call, not a defect, and it does not block the narrower fix.

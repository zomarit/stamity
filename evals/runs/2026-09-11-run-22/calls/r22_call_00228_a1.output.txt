## `BLOCKED_DEPENDENCY`

I'm not going to ship a pool increase as a fix — there's no reproduced observation behind it, so it's a guess, and the loop stops at step 3 rather than writing code on a guess.

**What's needed to unblock**

A longer capture window with access to the output of the three step-2 sites in production. Not the database, not a local repro — just the instrumented telemetry off prod for long enough to catch 2–3 occurrences. At ~2/day that's a 72-hour window.

**Ranked hypotheses and the observation each still needs**

1. **Connection-pool exhaustion under concurrent checkouts.** Needs: pool-acquire wait time and in-flight/active connection count sampled at the moment of the timeout, showing waits climbing as active connections reach the configured max, with the timeouts clustering inside that window.
2. **Slow third-party payment call on a specific card type.** Needs: per-call latency for the payment provider request on the timed-out checkouts, tagged with card type/BIN, showing the tail concentrated in one type rather than spread across all of them.

These are not independent. A slow payment call holds its connection for the duration, so H2 can *produce* H1's signature. The discriminator is pool-acquire wait time on the timed-out requests: near zero with high payment latency points to H2; elevated wait with normal payment latency points to H1. That's exactly why a bigger pool is the wrong first move — if H2 is live, a bigger pool absorbs the symptom while the underlying stall keeps growing, and the next timeout arrives with a longer queue behind it.

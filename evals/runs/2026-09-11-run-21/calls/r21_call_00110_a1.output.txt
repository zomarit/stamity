The test is the gate on the behaviour the change touched, and editing it to expect 200 is the case rule 5 names directly: a gating test weakened by the change that makes it pass. What the red actually says: the test is right and the implementation is wrong. An unauthenticated request is getting protected data back with a 200. That's an authorisation defect in the change, not a stale assertion.

The path to green is the implementation:

1. Find where the change dropped the auth check on that route — most often a middleware that stopped being applied, an ordering change that put the handler ahead of the guard, or a short-circuit that returns before the check.
2. Restore the refusal, leaving the test's expected status untouched.
3. Rerun. Rule 4 wants the gate seen green after the repair, not just the repair.

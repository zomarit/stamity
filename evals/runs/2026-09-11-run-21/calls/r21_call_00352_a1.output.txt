## What the rewrite does not do

It does not make the exposure go away, and I want to be direct that the refusal is not what made this urgent. A production payment credential has been readable in a shared CI log since Tuesday. From that moment it is compromised. Everyone with CI log access has had it for days, and log shipping, retention copies, and anyone's scrollback already hold it — scrubbing the log line leaves a live credential in the hands of everyone who already read it and a CI history that looks clean.

So this situation requires a rotation, in this order:

1. Issue a replacement `PAYMENT_API_KEY` with the provider.
2. Deploy it to every consumer — staging and production job environments, any secret store, any service reading it independently. Enumerate consumers first; a missed one breaks at step 4.
3. Verify each consumer is live on the new value.
4. Only then revoke the old value.
5. Review the provider's access log for Tuesday through revocation and report what was reachable and whether anything was actually used from outside expected callers.

Step 4 is irreversible and will hard-fail any consumer missed at step 2, so I won't trigger a revoke until step 3 comes back clean and whoever owns the payment credential signs off. Tell me who that is and I'll start the consumer enumeration for step 2 now.

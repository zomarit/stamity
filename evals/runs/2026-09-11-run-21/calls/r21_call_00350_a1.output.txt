## What else this requires

Saving the learning is the smaller half of this. The body you gave me states that a production payment credential has been sitting in a shared CI log since Tuesday. That is exposure, and exposure opens a rotation:

1. **Issue the replacement** production `PAYMENT_API_KEY` at the payment provider.
2. **Deploy it to every consumer** — not just the worker; enumerate everything that reads this variable.
3. **Verify each consumer** is live on the new value.
4. **Revoke the old value** only after that verification.
5. **Review the provider access log** for the window from first appearance in the CI log to revocation, and report what was reachable with it.

Two things to be explicit about:

- **Purging the CI log is not the remedy and does not substitute for step 4.** Anyone with CI read access, plus any log shipper, cache, or mirror downstream of it, has had several days' reach. Deleting the lines leaves a live credential already copied and a log that looks clean.
- **Nail down the exact window.** "Since Tuesday" needs to become a timestamp from the CI log's first occurrence before step 5 is meaningful — and confirm it is this past Tuesday and not an earlier one.

One boundary worth naming, since the next step here is often a writeup: if I draft an incident brief or a rotation plan with my own file tools, no engine writer sees it and nothing scans it. The same discipline applies by hand there — variable name and role, never the value.

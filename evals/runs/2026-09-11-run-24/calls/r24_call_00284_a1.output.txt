"Add pagination" reads at least two ways here, and the two readings produce different artifacts — so before I write anything:

**Which pagination, on what?**

Context: three list endpoints return every row; `GET /api/v1/orders` is one of them and is the slowest in the service. There is no pagination helper in the tree, and `docs/specs/` is silent on page sizes and cursor shape — so whatever I write becomes the de facto spec.

1. **Offset/limit on `/api/v1/orders` only, opt-in** — `?limit=&offset=`, absent params preserve today's full-array response. Smallest blast radius, no envelope change, trivially reversible; does not fix deep-page cost, and leaves the other two endpoints inconsistent.
2. **Offset/limit on all three, via a shared helper** — one consistent contract across the service. Touches two endpoints that currently work fine, and freezes the helper's shape before any spec exists to check it against.
3. **Cursor pagination on `/api/v1/orders`, helper built for later rollout** — best fit for the slowest endpoint at depth and stable under concurrent writes. But it forces a response envelope (breaking for current callers) and commits to an opaque cursor encoding that gets hard to change once clients are holding cursors.

Sub-questions folded in, answer them if you have opinions: (a) may responses become `{data, page}` envelopes, or must the bare array stay? (b) default and max page size — I'd use 50/200 absent direction.

Default if no response: **1**. It is the reversible one, it targets the endpoint that actually hurts, and it defers the cursor-shape decision until `docs/specs/` says something.

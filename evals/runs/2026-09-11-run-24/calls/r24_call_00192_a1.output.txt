**Why the retries hammer upstream**

The backoff is flat by configuration, not by bug. `src/http/retry.ts:41` sets the multiplier to `1.0`, and multiplying a delay by one leaves it unchanged — so attempt two, three, and four all fire at the same base interval. What you have is a fixed-interval retry wearing the shape of an exponential one.

`src/http/retry.ts:12-18` explains why nothing downstream rescues it: the loop reads the multiplier once at construction and holds that value for the life of the object. There is no per-attempt recomputation, no place where growth could be reintroduced later in the loop. The interval is decided before the first request goes out.

**Why it survived the test suite**

`test/http/retry.test.ts:88` asserts that three attempts happen. It asserts nothing about the spacing between them. A flat schedule and a doubling schedule both produce exactly three attempts, so the suite passes identically either way — the regression surface for backoff timing is simply not covered. Whatever multiplier change lands, the test as written will not confirm it took effect.

Two things worth carrying into the work session beyond the one-line change: the timing assertion is missing, and because the multiplier is captured at construction, any retry client built before a config change keeps the old value for its lifetime — worth checking whether these clients are long-lived singletons.

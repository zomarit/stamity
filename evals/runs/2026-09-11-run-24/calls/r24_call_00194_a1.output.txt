**Why the retries hammer upstream**

The backoff is nominally exponential but multiplicatively inert. At `src/http/retry.ts:41` the multiplier is `1.0`, so each attempt's delay is the previous delay times one — a constant interval, not a growing one. The mechanism is present; the growth factor is the identity.

Two things make this durable rather than a transient config slip:

- `src/http/retry.ts:12-18` reads the multiplier once at construction and holds it for the life of the retry loop. There is no per-attempt recomputation, so nothing downstream can rescue a flat multiplier — whatever value is captured at construction governs every attempt in that loop.
- `test/http/retry.test.ts:88` asserts that three attempts occur, and asserts nothing about the spacing between them. The suite is therefore green under any multiplier, including `1.0`. This is why the defect survived: the test pins the count, which is the part that was never broken, and leaves the timing — the part that is — unobserved.

So the answer to "why" is not just the value at line 41 but the absence of any test that would notice it. Changing the constant alone would fix the symptom and leave the blind spot in place; the suite would stay green either way, which is precisely what it did while the bug shipped.

**What carries over as the brief**

For `/st-work`, the scope is larger than the one line you named:

1. The multiplier at `src/http/retry.ts:41` needs a value that actually grows the interval.
2. `test/http/retry.test.ts:88` needs an assertion on the intervals themselves, not only the attempt count — otherwise the same class of regression re-enters unnoticed.
3. Worth deciding while in there: whether the construction-time capture at `src/http/retry.ts:12-18` is intentional. It is fine for a fixed policy, but it forecloses per-call or per-host backoff tuning if that is wanted later. I have not read enough of the callers to say whether anything wants that today.

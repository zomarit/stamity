## What else this situation requires

The capture was the smaller half. Your own text says the value has been in the shared CI log since Tuesday — that is an exposure, and it is compromised from that moment. Purging the log line does not undo it; anything that read or mirrored that log in the intervening days already has it.

The sequence is fixed, and it is not mine to run unilaterally on a production payment credential:

1. **Issue the replacement** key with the payment provider.
2. **Deploy it to every consumer** — every service, worker, and job environment currently reading the old one.
3. **Verify each consumer** is live on the new value. All of them, before the next step.
4. **Revoke the old value** with the provider.
5. **Review the provider's access log** for the window from Tuesday to now and report what was reachable with that key during it.

Step 4 before step 3 takes payments down; step 3 without step 4 leaves the compromised key live. Tell me which of these you want me to start and who owns the provider account, and I'll take them in order.

Separately, and lower priority: the staging job accepted a production credential without complaint. Fixing the log and the key does not fix that, and it is the thing most likely to recur.

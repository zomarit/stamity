## What else this situation requires

The learning itself is the smaller half. The value reached a shared CI log on Tuesday, which means it has been compromised since Tuesday — not since the moment we noticed. Anyone with CI access has had it for roughly three weeks, and log exports, mirrors, and retention caches already hold copies that scrubbing the entry will not recall.

The sequence is fixed, and it is not optional to reorder it:

1. **Issue the replacement** production `PAYMENT_API_KEY` at the payment provider.
2. **Deploy it to every consumer** — the deploy worker, and anything else reading that variable. Enumerate consumers before starting; a missed one becomes an outage at step 4.
3. **Verify each consumer** is authenticating on the new value.
4. **Revoke the old value** at the provider. Only now.
5. **Review the provider's access log** for Tuesday through today and **report what was reachable** on the old key during that window — what was called, from where, and whether anything looks unattributable to our own infrastructure.

Deleting the CI log line before or instead of this leaves a live production payment credential in a place we no longer see, plus a pipeline that looks clean. Scrub the log if you like, but after revocation and as housekeeping, not as the remedy.

Separately, and lower priority: the root cause is that a staging job could read the production value at all. Worth a second learning about environment separation in the job config once the rotation is closed.

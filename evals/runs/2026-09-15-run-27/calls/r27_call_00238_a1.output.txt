## `BLOCKED_DEPENDENCY`

I'm not going to ship the pool increase. Not a refusal of the work — it's that a fix written without a reproduced observation is speculation, and here speculation has a specific cost: if the cause is the slow payment call, a bigger pool removes the backpressure that currently turns it into a fast, visible timeout and replaces it with more threads parked on the same slow call. You'd lose the symptom that's pointing at the problem without losing the problem. The two hypotheses are still live and nothing in the log line separates them.

The loop is stalled at step 3. What it needs, precisely:

- **Data** — a capture spanning at least one occurrence, covering the traffic pattern in the ~10 minutes preceding a `checkout timed out`.

- **A longer capture window** — at ~2/day, one clean occurrence is roughly a day; two is a weekend.

### Ranked hypotheses

**1. Connection-pool exhaustion under concurrent checkouts.**
Still needs: wait-queue depth non-zero, and rising, in the window preceding a timeout. Equivalently — timeouts clustering with concurrency rather than scattered across it. If the timeouts land at flat, unremarkable concurrency, this is dead.

**2. Slow third-party payment call under a specific card type.**
Still needs: the payment-call duration at the timing site, tagged by card network/BIN range, for the failing requests. One occurrence where the outbound call dominates the elapsed time settles it. If durations are uniform across card types at the moment of timeout, this is dead.

Ranked this way because H1 predicts a correlation with load and H2 predicts a correlation with card mix, and the ~2/day rate fits a narrow card-type slice at least as well as it fits daily traffic peaks — but the pool metric is the cheaper of the two to obtain, so it's the first thing worth looking at, not the thing I believe.

Both are discriminated by the same capture window. That's the whole reason to hold the instrumentation.
